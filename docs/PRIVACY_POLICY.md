# Twelve Pits (Ayo): Privacy Policy

Last updated: 31 October 2026

Twelve Pits (Ayo) is a board game published by Neribo Group ("we", "us"). This policy explains what the game does with information. The short version: **it collects nothing, and it sends nothing to any server.**

## What we collect

Nothing. The game has:

- no account and no sign-in,
- no advertising,
- no analytics, tracking or crash-reporting tools,
- no way to connect to the internet. The app does not ask for the internet permission.

We do not collect, receive, sell or share any personal information.

## What stays on your phone

The game keeps a small amount of data **only on your phone**, so that it can work the way you expect:

- your settings (music, music volume, sound effects, vibration, seed speed),
- the player names and avatars you choose on the game setup screen and on the Two phones screen,
- the games won in each mode, and who starts the next game,
- the game you are playing against the computer or on one phone, so you can continue it later. A game played between two phones is never saved.

This data never leaves your phone. It is not backed up to the cloud by the app. You can clear it at any time by choosing Reset scores in Settings, or by clearing the app's storage or uninstalling the game in your phone's settings.

## Playing between two phones

The **Two phones** mode lets two people play on their own phones. For this, the game uses Bluetooth and Wi-Fi **only to talk directly to the other player's phone**, which must be close by. The connection is made by Google Play services (the Nearby Connections feature of your phone), and it works without the internet.

What is sent to the other phone, and only to that phone:

- the name and avatar you chose,
- the pit you tap, and a few messages that keep the two boards the same (for example "play again" and "I left the game").

Nothing is sent to us or to any server, and nothing is collected. Before a game starts, both phones show the same four-digit code so that you can check you are connected to the phone you meant to connect to. When the game ends or you leave the screen, the connection is closed. The game does not read, keep or send your location.

## Permissions

The game asks for **Vibrate**. It lets the phone vibrate during play when you switch Vibration on in Settings. Android does not show a question for this permission, and the game never uses it when Vibration is off.

For the Two phones mode, Android also asks you to allow the game to use Bluetooth and Wi-Fi nearby. The question appears only when you tap HOST A GAME or JOIN A GAME. The names Android shows depend on the version of Android:

- **Android 13 and newer:** "Nearby devices" (Bluetooth scan, connect and advertise, and Nearby Wi-Fi devices).
- **Android 12:** "Nearby devices", and on Android 12 also "Location".
- **Android 8 to 11:** Android calls the permission **Location**, because on these versions Android only lets an app search for other phones over Bluetooth with it. The game uses it only to find the other phone. It does not read, keep or send where you are. On these versions the Location switch of the phone must also be on while you look for the other phone.

If you say no, the Two phones mode cannot start, and everything else in the game keeps working. You can change your answer at any time in the phone's settings for the game.

## Children

The game does not collect any information from anyone, including children. It has no chat, no links to other sites and no purchases. In the Two phones mode the only things the two players can send each other are the name and avatar they chose and their moves.

## Changes to this policy

If the game changes in a way that affects your privacy, we will update this page and the "Last updated" date before the new version is released.
